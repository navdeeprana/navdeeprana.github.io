Onsager reciprocal relations

A discussion on
Author

Navdeep Rana

Published

November 15, 2022

Onsager reciprocal relations is a general statement about the irreversible processes. ORR are an application of two fundamental laws of equilibrium statistical mechanics to non-equilibrium systems which are not far away from the equilibrium. The two fundamental laws are : Boltzmann Distribution and the principle of time reversal symmetry.

Examples

Seeback and Peltier effects

When a temperature gradient is applied across a material, an electric potential difference arises between the two ends. This phenomena is called the Seeback effect. The relation between the temperature gradient \(\Delta T\) and the potential difference \(\Delta V\) is [ V = - S T, ] where \(S\) is the Seeback coefficient. The opposite phenomena, where a potential difference sets up a temperature gradient in the system is called the Peltier effect. [ = (_A - _B) I ] The Seeback and Peltier coefficients are related to each other as [ = T S ]

Interacting molecular species

Consider two interacting non-conserved order-parameter fields \(\phi_{1}(\symbf{r},t)\) and \(\phi_{2}(\symbf{r},t)\) with the following Landau-Ginzburg free energy form \[\begin{align}\label{eq:fe} \mathcal{F} = \int d^{d}r \left[ \frac{a}{2}\left(\phi_{1}^{2} + \phi_{2}^{2}\right) + \frac{b}{4}\left(\phi_{1}^{4} + \phi_{4}^{2}\right) + \frac{K}{2}\left(|\nabla\phi_{1}|^{2} + |\nabla\phi_{2}|^{2}\right) + \chi \phi_{1}\phi_{2} \right], \end{align}\] where \(\chi\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\) is the interaction term. Further, we have chosen identical doubled well potential for both \(\phi_{1}\) and \(\phi_{2}\). In principle, the exact form of the potential could vary, which can be modeled via choosing different \(a\) and \(b\) for \(\phi_{1}\) and \(\phi_{2}\). The equations of motion for \(\phi_{i}\)’s are \(\dot{\phi_{i}} = -\frac{\delta F}{\delta \phi_{i}}\) implies \[\begin{align}\label{eq:phi_dynamics} \dot{\phi_{1}} = -\frac{\delta F}{\delta \phi_{1}} &= K \nabla^{2}\phi_{1} - \left(a + b \phi_{1}^{2}\right)\phi_{1} - \chi \phi_{2}\\ \dot{\phi_{2}} = -\frac{\delta F}{\delta \phi_{2}} &= K \nabla^{2}\phi_{2} - \left(a + b \phi_{2}^{2}\right)\phi_{2} - \chi \phi_{1}, \end{align}\] where the coefficient of the cross terms \(\chi\) is the same.

Liquid Crystals

Proof

Consider a statistical system described by a set of \(N\) variables \(\left\{x_{i};~i = 1,2,\ldots,N\right\}\), with \(x_i \equiv x_i^{\circ}\) at the equilibrium. If the system is perturbed to a state that is not far from the equilibrium, it will relax back to it’s equilibrium state, setting up fluxes for various \(\left\{x_{i}\right\}\). Assuming that we are not far away from the equilibrium, we can write down the following linear flux-force relation \[\begin{align}\label{eq:forceflux} \dot{x_{i}} = \Gamma_{ij} X_{j}, \end{align}\] we will shortly describe what \(X_{j}\) are. We are interested in calculating average values of various quantities, for example \(x_{i}\), \(x_{i} X_{j}\). Since we are not far from equilibrium and the system is relaxing towards its equilibrium state, we can compute these averages using the machinery of equilibrium statistical mechanics. Essentially, the probability of a fluctuation which takes the system out of equilibrium is proportional to the number of microstates accessible by that fluctuation i.e. \[ P(\left\{x_{i}\right\}) \propto \Omega(\left\{x_{i}\right\}).\] Using the Boltzmann relation \(S = k \ln{\Omega}\), we get \(P\left(\left\{x_{i}\right\}\right) \propto e^{\Delta S/k}\) where \(\Delta S\) is the change in entropy. We have \[\begin{align} \Delta S &= S\left(x_{i}\right) - S\left(x^{\circ}_{i}\right) \\ &= \left(\frac{\partial S}{\partial x_{i}}\right)_{x_{i}=x_{i}^{\circ}}\Delta x_{i} \\ &+ \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2} S}{\partial x_{i}x_{j}}\right)_{x_{i,j}=x_{i,j}^{\circ}}\Delta x_{i} \Delta x_{j}, \end{align}\] where \(\Delta x_{i} = x_{i} - x_{i}^{\circ}\). Since the entropy is maximum at the equilibrium, the first derivative vanishes. We then have \[\begin{align}\label{eq:deltaS} \Delta S &= -\beta_{ij}\Delta x_{i} \Delta x_{j}, \end{align}\] where we have defined \[\begin{align} \beta_{ij} = -\beta_{ji} \equiv \frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial^{2} S}{\partial x_{i}x_{j}}\right)_{x_{i,j}=x_{i,j}^{\circ}} \end{align}\] From \(\eqref{eq:deltaS}\), the driving force is defined as \[\begin{align} X_{i} = \lim_{\Delta x_{i} \rightarrow 0} \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta x_{i}} = - \beta_{ij}\Delta x_{j} \end{align}\]

We have Q: Why can you still use the Boltzmann average here? \[\begin{align} \left< x_{i} X_{j}\right>=\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x_{i} X_{j} e^{\Delta S /k}\prod_{i} d x_{i}}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{\Delta S /k} \prod_{i} d x_{i}} \end{align}\] \[\begin{align}\label{eq:avexi} \left< x_{i}\right>=x_{i}^{\circ}=\frac{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}x_{i} e^{\Delta S /k}\prod_{i} d x_{i}}{\int_{-\infty}^{\infty} e^{\Delta S /k} \prod_{i} d x_{i}} \end{align}\] Differentiating \(\eqref{eq:avexi}\) with respect to \(x_{j}^{\circ}\) we arrive at

\[\left< x_{i} X_{j}\right>=-k \delta_{ij}\]

Q: What is the point of time flow when it is irreversible. Or reversible? From time-reversibility and homogeneity we have

\[\begin{align}\label{eq:avexi} \left<x_{i}(t)x_{j}(t+s)\right> &= \left<x_{i}(t)x_{j}(t-s) \right> \\ &= \left<x_{i}(t+s)x_{j}(t) \right> \end{align}\]

Subtracting \(\left<x_{i}(t)x_{j}(t)\right>\) from both sides and taking the limit \(s\rightarrow0\) we get the following

\[\begin{align} \lim_{s\rightarrow0}\left<x_{i}(t)\left[x_{j}(t+s)-x_{j}(t)\right]\right> &= \lim_{s\rightarrow0}\left<\left[x_{i}(t+s)-x_{i}(t)\right]x_{j}(t)\right> \\ \left<x_{i}(t)\dot{x_{j}}(t)\right> &= \left<\dot{x_{i}}(t)x_{j}(t) \right> \\ \end{align}\] Using \(\eqref{eq:forceflux}\) we arrive at \[\begin{align}\label{eq:trsresult} \Gamma_{jl}\left<x_{i}(t)X_{l}(t)\right> &= \Gamma_{il}\left<x_{j}(t)X_{l}(t)\right> \\ \end{align}\]

Time reversibility will only take us this far, to proceed further, we need to compute \(\left<x_{i}(t)X_{j}(t)\right>\).Using the above expression in \(\eqref{eq:trsresult}\) we get \[\begin{align}\label{eq:avexi} \Gamma_{ij} = \Gamma_{ji}, \end{align}\] which are the Onsager Reciprocal relations.

Onsager Symmetry relations as a Fluctuation-Dissipation Theorem

Since the Symmetry of the kinetic coefficients is derived from the fundamental hypothesis of the equilibrium statistical mechanics. It can be viewed as a relation between the systematic behaviour of the system to a Fluctuation with the random part. To elaborate, the admittance is derived from the properties of the fluctuations.

Minimal way to break ORR

Let’s add general linear terms to the equations of motion of \(\phi_{1}\) and \(\phi_{2}\) \[\begin{align}\label{eq:fe} \dot{\phi_{1}} &= K \nabla^{2}\phi_{1} - \left(a + b \phi_{1}^{2}\right)\phi_{1} - \chi \phi_{2} + A \phi_{1} + B \phi_{2}\\ \dot{\phi_{2}} &= K \nabla^{2}\phi_{2} - \left(a + b \phi_{2}^{2}\right)\phi_{2} - \chi \phi_{1} + C \phi_{1} + D \phi_{2}. \end{align}\] Since all our coefficients are phenomenological, we can combine the \(A,D\) terms with the \(a\) terms and rewrite the equations as \[\begin{align}\label{eq:fe} \dot{\phi_{1}} &= K \nabla^{2}\phi_{1} - \left(a_{1} + b \phi_{1}^{2}\right)\phi_{1} + \left(-\chi + B\right) \phi_{2} \\ \dot{\phi_{2}} &= K \nabla^{2}\phi_{2} - \left(a_{2} + b \phi_{2}^{2}\right)\phi_{2} + \left(-\chi + C\right) \phi_{1}, \end{align}\] where \(a_{1} = a + A\) and \(a_{2} = a + D\). Further, rewriting in terms of \(\overline{\chi} = -\chi + \frac{B}{2} + \frac{C}{2}\) and \(\alpha = \frac{B}{2} - \frac{C}{2}\), we get \[\begin{align}\label{eq:fe} \dot{\phi_{1}} &= K \nabla^{2}\phi_{1} + \left(a_{1} + b \phi_{1}^{2}\right)\phi_{1} + \left(\overline{\chi} + \alpha\right) \phi_{2} \\ \dot{\phi_{2}} &= K \nabla^{2}\phi_{2} + \left(a_{2} + b \phi_{2}^{2}\right)\phi_{2} + \left(\overline{\chi} - \alpha\right) \phi_{1}. \end{align}\] Notice that the structure of the terms except the \(\alpha\) terms is exactly identical to \(\eqref{eq:phi_dynamics}\). Thus, the minimal way to break the ORR relations for systems which are amenable to a Landau-Ginzburg like free energy description is by adding antisymmetric terms.
